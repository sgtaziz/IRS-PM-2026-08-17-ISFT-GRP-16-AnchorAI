## Full answers

*AI-simulated persona: "Nurul Huda" (pseudonym), Kampong Collective, Toa Payoh. Not a real person.*

## Q1. What is your role, and how many members does your club or chain serve?
I'm the membership and community lead at Kampong Collective, a class-heavy gym in Toa Payoh. We have around 900 members. I'm not the owner. I run the front desk team, which is three of us, plus the class timetable, which is 28 classes a week, and onboarding for new members. I started here as a Zumba instructor six years ago, so I still think like an instructor. About 60% of our visits are group classes: HIIT, spin, Muay Thai, yoga, Zumba, Strong Mums, and Silver Strength for the 55 and above.

## Q2. How do you find out today that a member is about to leave? What do you do about it?
Honestly, I notice with my eyes first. I know the regulars by face, so when the 7am spin group is a person short, I feel it. I also keep a paper list behind the desk of "people I haven't seen". I write names down when I notice and cross them off when they're back. Then I WhatsApp them myself, something like "Hey, we missed you at Tuesday HIIT, everything ok?" Only if they ignore two or three messages do I check the system for their last visit. The system is a backup for me, not the first signal.

## Q3. A regular who came twice a week drops by a fifth or more this month. Would you act on that? After how long?
A fifth, no, not really. Twice a week to maybe once-and-a-bit is normal. People travel, work gets busy, kids are sick. For me it's not the percentage, it's missing their regular class two weeks running. If someone who's always at Thursday spin isn't there for two Thursdays in a row, I send a message. One week I let go. A drop of 20% across a month I wouldn't even see unless someone showed me a report. A 50% drop that lasts three or four weeks, I'd pay attention to.

## Q4. When are new members most at risk: the first month, the first three months, or later?
First month, and honestly the first two to three weeks. If a new member hasn't booked a class or come twice by week three, they're the biggest risk I have. They feel lost, they don't know anyone, and nobody has said hi to them. By three months, if they've found a class and a face they know, they usually stay. I'd guess maybe a quarter to a third of the people who join and never book a class are gone within three months. Those who attend a class in the first two weeks are much stickier. There's also a dip around months four to six when the new-year or promo crowd runs out of steam, but that's less than the early drop.

## Q5. How low does attendance have to fall before you treat someone as disengaged?
It depends on the person. For someone who came three times a week, once a week is already a change, but they're not gone. I treat someone as disengaged when they've missed their usual class two weeks in a row and haven't told anyone. Or, in a rougher number, nothing for three weeks. For a new member, two weeks without a visit is already bad. Someone who only ever came once a week, I'd give four weeks before I worry. I really don't have one number. I look at what's normal for that person.

## Q6. Do members on 6- or 12-month contracts leave at renewal? How far ahead of the end date would you reach out?
Yes, the renewal point is when people finally decide. They've been half-gone for weeks and then the reminder arrives and they cancel. I don't wait for the reminder. The bad time is the two months before the end, when they're coming less. I'd try a personal check-in about 6 to 8 weeks before the end date, then the renewal reminder itself at about 3 to 4 weeks. For people who are still coming regularly, the reminder is enough. Month-to-month members are different: they can leave any time, so there's no neat date, and that's where the owner sometimes approves a discount.

## Q7. When an instructor leaves, do their regulars follow them out? Can you give an example? How long after does it show?
Yes, definitely, and it hurt. Last year our Muay Thai coach left. The class basically moved with him. Maybe a third of the regulars stopped within a month. The ones we kept were the ones who already did other classes here, like HIIT or spin, so they had other reasons to come. The Muay Thai-only people mostly went to follow him or just stopped. It showed up quickly, within two to four weeks, and then a little more trickled away over the next month or two.

## Q8. Same question for a cancelled class or time slot.
A cancelled class is quieter but real. We moved a popular 7pm class to 8pm once. Nobody complained to me, and that's the dangerous part. Over six to eight weeks we lost several members, quietly, because 8pm didn't fit their dinner or their commute. If we cancel a class outright, the people who only came for that slot are the most at risk, and I see it after about 2 to 4 weeks when they've tried other things and nothing fits. If they have other classes they like, they usually adjust.

## Q9. For a new member who is slipping, what works best: a call, a free PT session, something else?
For new members, a free PT session can work, because many of them feel lost on the gym floor. They don't know the machines and they're shy. A session or a floor orientation helps them. But before that, a personal message and an invitation to a specific beginner-friendly class works really well, because it gives them a face. A call is OK, but young people don't pick up. WhatsApp first. If they're a friend-joiner, bring the friend along to something.

## Q10. For a long-time regular who is fading?
Not a PT session. That feels salesy to someone who's been in our classes for two years and knows what they're doing. For them, a personal check-in works best: "we missed you, is everything ok?" Often the reason is something ordinary, like injury, a new baby, or a work change. If it's the class or the timetable, I can sometimes help. I'd also check whether their class friends are still coming. Often when a friend stops, they stop. A discount is the last thing I'd offer a long-time regular.

## Q11. Do social offers (bring a friend, group challenges) work for members who joined with a friend?
Yes, mostly. Bring-a-friend passes work very well for social members, especially the class gang types. It works because they're not coming for the gym, they're coming for each other. But if the friend has already left, a bring-a-friend pass to the one who remains is a bit sad, so I'd check first who is still coming. Group challenges work for some, mainly the competitive ones. Quiet people who come alone don't like being pushed into it. Friend pairs are a risk too: when one drops out, the other usually follows within a month or two.

## Q12. When do you give discounts, and to whom do you never give them?
I don't like discounts. The people who stay for a discount leave for the next discount. The owner sometimes approves one, mostly for month-to-month members who are about to cancel and say money is the reason. I'd never give a discount to a long-time class regular who is fading for other reasons, because it doesn't fix anything and it tells the others they can ask. I also wouldn't give one to a member who's just on holiday or injured. And never to someone who joined on a promo and is only here for the cheaper price.

## Q13. Which channel gets answered: SMS, email, phone or in person? Does that depend on age or offer?
It depends on age. Under 40, WhatsApp or SMS; they reply within a day. 55 and above, I call. They like a real voice, and the Silver Strength members would find a text a bit cold. Email is basically for receipts; hardly anyone reads our emails, maybe one in ten. In person at the desk is the best of all, but only works if they still come in. So for fading members it's the message that does the job, and for the ones who are gone, a call.

## Q14. How often can you contact the same member before it backfires? (Give a number: once per how many days? How many offers in, say, 3 months?)
I keep to about one personal message a month unless they reply. Once they reply, it's a conversation and then it's fine. We had complaints once when a member got a promo SMS, a check-in and a renewal reminder in the same fortnight. They felt hounded. So three touches in two weeks is too much. For offers, I'd say no more than one offer in three months for the same person, and a second only if the first clearly wasn't the problem.

## Q15. Are there members you would never spend a paid offer on?
Yes. People who are already coming regularly, because you're just giving money away. People who told us they're leaving for a reason an offer can't fix, like moving overseas. Those on holiday, injured or pregnant, who have said so. Also people who've already had an offer recently. And I'd think twice about discount-chasers, the ones who joined on a promo and have never booked a class. Money would be better spent on a person's time than on them.

## Q16. Any rule you must follow: data protection, head office policy, contract terms?
We're careful about the PDPA, Singapore's data protection law. We only message people for the reasons they signed up for, and anyone who opts out of marketing gets no promos. A check-in is a grey area, so I keep those personal and non-promotional. The owner has to approve any discount, and I can't change contract terms or freeze a membership without the owner's agreement. Contracts have set cancellation periods, and I can't promise anything that breaks them. I'm not a lawyer, so on the details I'd ask the owner.

## Q17. If a tool gave your staff a weekly call list with reasons, what would make you trust it, and what would make you ignore it?
I'd trust it if each name comes with a reason my staff can say out loud, like "missed Tuesday HIIT two weeks running" or "new member, no class booked yet". Not "risk score 0.82". I'd also want to be able to skip someone I know is on holiday or injured, and have the tool remember that. And a short list, maybe 10 to 15 names a week, not 80. I'd ignore it if the list is long, if it keeps showing people I already know about, or if it writes robot messages. My worry is it turns my personal touch into robot messages. I'd want it to suggest and let us write the words. And if it suggested a discount for a regular, I'd stop using it.

## Q18. Anything we should have asked?
Ask about friends and groups. Most tools look at one person at a time, but in a class gym people leave in clusters. If one in a friend pair stops, the other is at risk, and if a coach leaves, the whole class is. Also ask what staff do when a member says "I'm busy", because that's the most common answer and it often means "I feel awkward coming back". The tool should help staff make coming back easy, not just send an offer. And please check with staff how much time they actually have. My team of three can't do more than a handful of calls a day.
